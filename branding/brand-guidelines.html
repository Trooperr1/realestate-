Real Estate

01 · Positioning

Quiet confidence, precisely built.

Cofimo represents property at the highest tier of craft and address. The brand does not shout — it is composed the way its listings are composed: with restraint, exact proportion, and nothing left to chance. Every surface, from a signature to a signboard, should read as considered rather than decorated.

The identity is built entirely in black and white. This is not a limitation — it is the point. A monochrome system reads as timeless, confident, and unmistakably premium, and it keeps every property photograph — not the logo — as the most colorful thing on the page.

02 · Why This Logo

Every choice, argued.

Nothing in the mark is decorative. Each element was chosen to do a specific job — signal trust, survive at small sizes, or keep the brand out of the way of the properties it represents. Here is the reasoning behind each decision.

A serif wordmark

Serif type is the visual shorthand private banks, auction houses, and law firms have used for a century to signal permanence — think Sotheby’s, not a listings app. For a brand selling trust as much as square footage, that association is worth borrowing deliberately.

Wide letter-spacing

Cofimo’s product is space — a cramped wordmark would undercut the pitch. Generous tracking is the typographic equivalent of an uncluttered floor plan: it reads as unhurried, which reads as expensive.

The circle monogram

A ring is the oldest architectural symbol of an opening — a window, an oculus, a column in plan view. The monogram is built to double as a small piece of architecture, not just a shrunken logo, so it still feels intentional at 32px on a favicon or embossed into a business card.

The diamond mark

One rotated square, used once, reads as a cut gem facet — precision and rarity in a single stroke. It is never repeated as a pattern; the moment it multiplies, it stops being a punctuation mark and starts being wallpaper.

Black and white, no exceptions

Every property has its own material palette — marble, brass, walnut, glass. A colored logo competes with that; a monochrome one never does. It also photographs identically on paper, on screen, and engraved into metal signage, and it won’t date the way a trend color will.

Illustration over stock photography

Where imagery is needed before real listing photography exists, the system uses a single hand-drawn motif — a dusk villa silhouette — instead of generic stock photos. It keeps every post on-brand from day one, and reads as considered rather than placeholder-grade.

04 · Clear Space & Misuse

Give it room to breathe.

Clear space

Maintain a minimum margin equal to the height of the diamond mark on every side of the wordmark. Nothing — text, imagery, edge of frame — should enter this zone.

Minimum size

Do not reproduce the full wordmark narrower than 160px / 1.5in. Below that width, use the monogram alone.

× Do not recolor the mark — black and white only, no gradients or tints.
× Do not condense, stretch, or rotate the wordmark.
× Do not place the black wordmark on a busy or mid-tone photograph.
× Do not substitute the serif wordmark with a different typeface.

05 · Color Palette

Two colors. No exceptions.

Cofimo Black and Cofimo White carry the entire system. An off-white and a soft graphite are permitted as backgrounds and secondary text — never as a substitute for the black or a stand-in accent color.

Usage ratio: 60–70% neutral ground (black or white), 25–35% the opposing neutral for type and line work, up to 5% graphite for supporting captions. Never introduce a third hue.

06 · Typography

Serif for authority. Sans for clarity.

Headlines and the wordmark are set in a classic serif at generous tracking. Supporting copy, labels, and UI text are set in a clean grotesque sans, always in small, evenly tracked caps for eyebrows and captions.
